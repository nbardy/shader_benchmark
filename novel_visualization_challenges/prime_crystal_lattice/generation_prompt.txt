**Objective**
Visualize **prime numbers as crystallographic defects** in a 3D cubic lattice, where each prime creates a unique disruption pattern in an otherwise perfect crystal structure.

**Mathematical recipe**

1. Create a 50×50×50 cubic lattice of atoms at integer coordinates.
2. For each prime p < 125000:
   - Place a "defect core" at position (p mod 50, (p//50) mod 50, p//2500)
   - Create vacancy patterns: remove atoms at positions where |pos - core| < √p/10
   - Generate strain field: displace nearby atoms by vector v = (pos - core) × sin(√p)/|pos - core|²
3. Twin primes create connected defect channels with enhanced strain fields.
4. Mersenne primes (2ⁿ-1) generate spiral dislocation patterns with helical symmetry.

**Styling**

* Atoms as metallic spheres (radius 0.15 units), silver base color
* Defect regions: color temperature gradient from blue (small primes) to white-hot (large primes)
* Strain visualization: atoms colored by displacement magnitude (green=low, red=high)
* Volumetric glow around defect cores with intensity ∝ 1/√p
* Enable subsurface scattering for crystalline appearance
* Camera at (80, 60, 40), looking at (25, 25, 25); FOV 35°
* Dark background with rim lighting from three sources
* Resolution 2400 × 2400 px, 16× SSAA for clean edges

**Deliverable** Single PNG showing prime distribution as crystal lattice defects
