TARGETED FOLLOW-UP — REPLACE THE GENERIC MACRO REPRESENTATION
==============================================================

USER REVIEW
-----------

“It is still a very generic base of like four normal-ass bricks that are
weirdly oval. Do dynamic morphing or bending into a real parrot form of the
base shapes. Fix that and make it not a lame parrot, but a real dynamic shape.”

Treat that review as a rejection of the prior macro-form acceptance decision.
The previous result failed at representation, not parameter tuning. Its body,
neck, head, wing, face, beak lobes, and feathers all reduce to independently
placed ellipsoids; its anatomy study changed scale, lean, reach, and placement
inside that same topology. Do not spend this run moving, resizing, recoloring,
or polishing that topology.

WHAT MAY SURVIVE
----------------

Preserve only the useful camera and framing observations, yellow/blue/ivory/
blue-black palette hierarchy, eye and face as focal region, differentiated
material-light pipeline, canopy depth, and the idea of parent-relative feather
flow. The supplied seed shader is implementation evidence and a negative
control, not a geometry template that must be retained.

ANATOMY-RESET GATE
------------------

Rebuild the parrot around a continuous, dynamic anatomical gesture. The torso,
neck, crown, and lower-body/tail flow must be designed through a changing shape
along a curved axis—not assembled from complete ovals. Viable families include
a varying-radius sweep around a spine C(s) with a transported frame and
asymmetric width/depth profiles; bent or twisted superquadrics with nonlinear
domain deformation; lofted cross-sections; or a deliberately carved hybrid.
Use bend, taper, twist, asymmetric profiles, smooth joins, intersections, and
subtractions where they create visible character.

The head must grow out of a neck transition rather than sit as a sphere above
an egg. Build a crown slope, nape/back curve, cheek cut, and forward thrust. The
upper beak must taper into a swept or carved hook with concave mouth negative
space and a distinct lower mandible—not three overlapping ovals. The wing must
be a thin curved shell or swept sheet rooted at the shoulder, tangent to and
wrapping around the torso before peeling down/back—not another blue volume
glued beside it.

SEARCH AND EVIDENCE
-------------------

Use the two mandatory whole-scene tournament passes for structural anatomy:

1. Pass 1: six genuinely different macro representation families. At least
   four cells must use different construction/topology, not one shared map fed
   by six parameter vectors. Make the cells neutral clay or solid silhouette;
   feathers, bokeh, and surface polish may not camouflage the anatomy.
2. Pass 2: develop the two strongest families under clay lighting that reveals
   curvature, attachment, thickness, carved negative space, and overlap. Keep
   alternatives structurally distinct; do not collapse into a local parameter
   sweep.

The seed baseline is allowed to lose. If every candidate still decomposes into
obvious eggs, capsules, or rounded bricks, reject the pass and render a wider
search. Do not interpret “least bad” as success. Use the remaining finals to
integrate the selected dynamic anatomy, then restore palette, materials,
atmosphere, and restrained feather structure without regressing its silhouette.

VISIBLE ACCEPTANCE TESTS
------------------------

- At thumbnail scale or in solid black, the silhouette reads immediately as a
  dynamic macaw rather than a pear or snowman with a beak.
- Crown → nape → back → rump forms one asymmetric flowing curve without an
  obvious head/neck/body seam.
- The breast changes width and direction along a curved axis, with a proud keel
  and lower taper; it is not a rotated ellipse.
- The head thrusts forward while the neck/body create a readable S-curve.
- The beak has a tapering hook, carved concavity, and mouth gap.
- The wing has thin-shell thickness, shoulder attachment, torso-conforming
  curvature, a swept trailing edge, and partial wrap/occlusion.
- A clay render shows changing surface curvature and attachment—not merely a
  stronger color silhouette.

Before every rewrite, state in revision_critique: (1) which generic oval/brick
decomposition is still visible, (2) which structural operation replaces it,
and (3) what silhouette or clay-shading evidence will prove the change. Render
at least four distinct successful FINAL revisions after the study passes, and
submit only a post-reset revision that passes the tests above.
