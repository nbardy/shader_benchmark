====================================================================

This follow-up doubles down on the successful representation change instead of

CAUSAL FAILURE TO REMOVE
------------------------

The seed's wing is evaluated in a warped local `wp` coordinate system, but its
feather roots are generated as an absolute world-space row/column grid. Each
feather is then rotated only in world XY. The last revision merely intersects
these independently placed shapes with the wing envelope. That clipping stops
tips from floating outside the panel, but it does not make root position,
orientation, camber, scale, overlap, foreshortening, or depth follow the wing.
The result is a blue slab with a decorative vertical appliqué.

Do not treat another world-space `center = vec3(...)`, `rotZ(p-center, angle)`,
or `max(feather, wingSdf-offset)` as surface attachment. Containment may remain
as a final safety bound, but it cannot be the construction.

USE THE ENFORCED FOUR-STUDY LADDER AS A RECURSIVE PART HIERARCHY
---------------------------------------------------------------

The four studies are dependent public experiments. Each pass must be rendered
and inspected. A later study must call the selected representation from every
earlier study; do not reimplement it with cheaper ovals.

STUDY 1 — INDIVIDUAL FEATHER

Render one enlarged identity-carrying feather per cell. Compare genuinely
different continuous constructions with a narrow embedded root, off-center
shoulder, changing left/right vane width, longitudinal bend, changing
thickness/camber, and a tapered or subtly notched tip. A capsule, ellipsoid,
diamond, rod, or pointed oval cannot be the full visible feather. Use a
longitudinal coordinate s and a continuous inverse-bend/profile or swept-curve
field. The result must expose a callable local-space feather function.

STUDY 2 — INTRINSIC FEATHER COAT

Build a neutral gently curved sheet using the selected feather function. The
sheet lives in intrinsic coordinates (u,v), not world XY. Compare at least
three curved staggered courses: short rounded shoulder coverts, medium coverts,
and longer/narrower outer feathers. Roots should be covered by the preceding
course; downstream courses overlap roughly 25–50% of exposed length. Across
the field, the flow must fan visibly by at least 25–40 degrees. Variation in
length, yaw, roll, curvature, spacing, and overlap must be smooth and correlated
with (u,v), with only sparse low-amplitude irregularity. No straight top root
row, parallel vertical comb, alternating diamond seams, or random scattering.

STUDY 3 — WING SURFACE FRAME AND WRAP

Map the selected coat onto the existing wing as a child of that wing. Define a
stable wing parameterization and frame:

    P(u,v) = point on the shoulder-to-tip wing surface
    Tu = normalize(dP/du)
    Tv = normalize(dP/dv)
    N  = normalize(cross(Tu,Tv))

An equivalent analytic or finite-difference frame is acceptable. A transported
frame along a wing centerline plus a cross-wing coordinate is also acceptable.
What matters is that every feather root is computed as P(u_i,v_i), its local
basis is derived from Tu/Tv/N, and its bent body remains close to the changing
surface before lifting at the tip. Parameterize the same geometry used by the
wing SDF; do not invent an unrelated decorative plane in front of it.

For the seed's current warped ellipsoid, this is one viable starting scaffold
(adapt signs/ranges only after rendering it):

    yl = 1.03*v
    shoulder = 1 + 0.18*smoothstep(-0.20, 0.70, yl)
    xw = 0.57*shoulder*u
    zw = -0.19*sqrt(max(1-u*u-v*v, epsilon))
    P = vec3(0.08 + xw + 0.17*yl - 0.075*yl*yl,
             -0.28 + yl,
             -0.43 + zw - 0.10*xw)

Apply the wing's existing shoulder/trailing cuts in the same domain. Finite
differences of P are preferable to pretending world Z is the normal. A feather
may then be evaluated as a curved UV planform plus height above P, or through a
root frame whose normal/camber is updated along its length.

Make the atlas diagnostically legible: show root markers and tangent/flow cues
or use controlled course colors where useful. Across all cells reject roots
outside the shoulder/wing, exposed bases against the yellow body/background,
equal-width edge feathers, and axes that remain parallel as the surface turns.
Feathers approaching the far/turning edge should become narrower, more
overlapped, darker or more occluded through real 3D orientation—not painted
screen-space shading.

STUDY 4 — HIERARCHICAL RELATIONSHIPS IN THE WHOLE BIRD

Render the complete parrot in every cell and vary only meaningful wing-coat
relationships: shoulder concealment beneath the neck, covert-to-flight-feather
transition, course depth ordering, wrap around the torso, and how two to four
long primaries participate in the lower/trailing silhouette. Preserve the
seed's torso, bill, cheek, camera, palette, and background. Reject an interior
feather badge, a disconnected lower spike, a straight curtain, or any option
that replaces the successful macro anatomy.

FINAL INTEGRATION AND TESTS
---------------------------

Use the remaining renders for at least six distinct full-frame final revisions.
Before each rewrite, name which parent→child relationship is still wrong, the
coordinate or geometry operation that replaces it, and the visible falsification
test. Iterate from largest dependency to smallest: wing frame → course field →
feather root/frame → continuous feather profile → material refinement.

The final shader must expose a live dependency chain equivalent to:

    torso gesture
      → shoulder-rooted wing surface P(u,v), Tu, Tv, N
        → curved course field in (u,v)
          → per-root transported feather frame
            → continuous bent/tapered feather profile

Visible acceptance tests:

- At least 75% of roots are hidden by upstream feathers or the shoulder.
- Three or more staggered courses remain visible, with coherent over/under
  ordering and no X-shaped or diamond-grid intersections.
- Neighbor directions change gradually, while the whole coat fans by at least
  25–40 degrees instead of forming a vertical comb.
- Edge-turning feathers visibly foreshorten and overlap more than central ones.
- Only two to four designed primaries break the wing silhouette; no random tip
  escapes and the coat is not merely a centered badge.
- In a single-color or thumbnail read, feathers and wing form one wrapped mass,
  not a blue panel plus pasted objects.
- The seed's dynamic S-curve, hooked bill, face assembly, framing, and focal
  hierarchy do not regress.

Submit the strongest historical final revision, not automatically the newest.
If the recursive coat fails, restore the strongest prior final and use the
remaining budget to correct the earliest broken dependency rather than adding
texture, color noise, or cosmetic polish.
